What is a crown or bridge?

A crown is a cap that covers a weak, broken or root-canal-treated tooth, restoring its shape, strength and appearance.

A bridge replaces one or more missing teeth by anchoring false teeth to crowns on the teeth either side of the gap. Both are fixed — you don't take them out.

When you may need a crown or bridge

  • After root canal treatment, especially on back teeth
  • A tooth that is cracked, badly broken or heavily filled
  • Teeth worn down by grinding
  • Badly shaped or discoloured teeth
  • One or more missing teeth (bridge)
  • To cover a dental implant

For a missing tooth, a bridge and an implant are both good options. A bridge is quicker; an implant doesn't involve the neighbouring teeth. We'll help you compare.

Aftercare tips

  • Avoid sticky or very hard foods on the temporary crown.
  • Brush and floss normally — use a floss threader or interdental brush under a bridge.
  • Don't bite very hard objects like ice, bones or nut shells.
  • If you grind your teeth, ask about a night guard.
  • Keep up regular check-ups and cleanings.

Frequently asked questions

Which crown material is best?

Zirconia and E-max look the most natural and are metal-free — ideal for front teeth. Metal-ceramic crowns are strong and economical. We recommend based on the tooth's position, your bite and budget.

How long do crowns and bridges last?

With good care, crowns and bridges often last 10–15 years or more.

Is getting a crown painful?

No. The tooth is numbed while it's prepared. Some sensitivity for a few days afterwards is normal.

Should I choose a bridge or an implant?

An implant replaces the tooth without touching the neighbouring teeth and protects the jawbone. A bridge is quicker and avoids surgery. Your dentist will explain which suits you better.
