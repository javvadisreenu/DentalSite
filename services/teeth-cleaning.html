What is teeth cleaning and scaling?

Even with good brushing, plaque slowly hardens into tartar (calculus) that a toothbrush can't remove. Tartar irritates the gums, causing bleeding, bad breath and, over time, gum disease that can loosen teeth.

Professional cleaning — scaling and polishing — removes tartar above and just below the gum line with an ultrasonic scaler, then polishes the teeth smooth so plaque sticks less.

Signs you're due for a cleaning

  • Gums that bleed when you brush or floss
  • Bad breath that doesn't go away
  • Yellow or brown deposits near the gum line
  • Red, puffy or tender gums
  • Stains from tea, coffee, paan or smoking
  • More than six months since your last cleaning

Most people need a professional cleaning every six months. If you have gum disease, we may recommend a deep cleaning (root planing) or more frequent visits.

Frequently asked questions

Does scaling damage tooth enamel?

No. This is a common myth. Ultrasonic scalers remove hardened tartar, not enamel. Teeth may feel different afterwards simply because the tartar that covered them is gone.

Does teeth cleaning hurt?

For most people it is painless. You may feel vibration and some sensitivity, especially if there is a lot of tartar. Numbing gel can be used if you are sensitive.

Why do my gums bleed after cleaning?

Inflamed gums can bleed briefly during and after cleaning. As the tartar is removed and gums heal, bleeding usually stops within a few days.

Will cleaning make my teeth white?

Cleaning removes surface stains, so teeth look brighter, but it doesn't change their natural shade. For that, ask about teeth whitening.
