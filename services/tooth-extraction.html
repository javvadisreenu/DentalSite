What is Tooth Extraction?

Sometimes a tooth is too damaged to save, is crowding other teeth, or is a wisdom tooth that can't come through properly. Removing it protects your other teeth and stops the pain or infection.

A simple extraction is used for a tooth that is fully visible in the mouth. A surgical extraction is used for a tooth that is broken at the gum line or impacted (stuck under the gum or bone), like many wisdom teeth.

When a tooth may need to be removed

  • Severe decay or infection that can't be fixed with a root canal
  • Painful, swollen or impacted wisdom teeth
  • A tooth broken below the gum line
  • Advanced gum disease that has loosened the tooth
  • Crowding before braces or other orthodontic treatment
  • Baby teeth that haven't fallen out on time

We always try to save a tooth first. Extraction is recommended only when it is the best option for your long-term oral health.

The Procedure

  1. Examination and X-rayAn OPG or CBCT scan shows the tooth's roots and nearby nerves, especially for wisdom teeth.
  2. Local anaesthesiaThe area is fully numbed. Sedation is available for anxious patients.
  3. RemovalA simple extraction gently loosens and lifts the tooth. A surgical extraction may need a small opening in the gum, and the tooth may be removed in sections.
  4. Stitches and gauzeDissolvable stitches are placed if needed, and you bite on gauze to control bleeding.
  5. Healing guidanceYou go home with clear aftercare instructions and a review appointment if needed.

Aftercare tips

  • Bite firmly on the gauze for 30–45 minutes.
  • For the first 24 hours: no spitting, vigorous rinsing, straws or smoking — let the blood clot form.
  • Use a cold pack on the cheek on and off during the first day.
  • Eat soft, cool foods and chew on the other side.
  • From the next day, rinse gently with warm salt water after meals.
  • Call us if bleeding doesn't stop, pain gets worse after day 3, or you develop a fever.

Frequently asked questions

Does tooth extraction hurt?

You won't feel pain during the procedure because the area is fully numbed — only some pressure. Mild soreness afterwards is normal and is managed with prescribed medicines.

Should I remove wisdom teeth that don't hurt?

Not always. We recommend removal only if a wisdom tooth is impacted, decaying, causing gum infections or damaging the tooth next to it. An X-ray helps decide.

What is a dry socket?

It happens when the blood clot in the socket is lost too early, exposing the bone. It causes throbbing pain 2–4 days after extraction. Following the aftercare steps — especially no smoking or straws — greatly lowers the risk. If it happens, we can treat it quickly.

How can I replace an extracted tooth?

Options include a dental implant, a bridge or a removable denture. We'll explain which suits you best, ideally before the extraction.
