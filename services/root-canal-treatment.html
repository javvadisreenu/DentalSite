What is a Root Canal?

Inside every tooth is soft tissue called the pulp, which holds the tooth's nerves and blood vessels. When deep decay, a crack or an old filling lets bacteria reach the pulp, it becomes inflamed or infected — and that is what causes the intense, lingering toothache.

Root canal treatment (RCT) removes the infected pulp, cleans and shapes the canals inside the root, then fills and seals them. It relieves the pain and, most importantly, lets you keep your natural tooth instead of having it removed.

Signs you may need a root canal

  • Lingering pain or sensitivity to hot or cold, even after the food is gone
  • Sharp pain when biting or chewing
  • A throbbing toothache that wakes you at night
  • Swollen or tender gums, or a pimple-like bump near one tooth
  • A tooth that has turned dark or grey
  • A deep cavity, a cracked tooth or a large old filling

Not every toothache needs a root canal. A quick examination and digital X-ray tell us exactly what is going on.

The Procedure

  1. Examination and digital X-rayWe check the tooth and see how far the infection has spread.
  2. Local anaesthesiaThe area is fully numbed — you feel pressure, not pain.
  3. Cleaning and shapingThe infected pulp is removed and the canals are cleaned with rotary files. An apex locator measures the exact canal length.
  4. Filling and sealingThe canals are filled with a biocompatible material and sealed to keep bacteria out.
  5. CrownA crown protects and strengthens the tooth — usually recommended for back teeth.

Aftercare tips

  • Don't eat until the numbness wears off, so you don't bite your cheek or tongue.
  • Avoid chewing hard food on that side until the permanent crown is fitted.
  • Mild tenderness for a few days is normal — take medicines as prescribed.
  • Keep brushing and flossing as usual.
  • Call us if you have swelling, severe pain, or if the temporary filling comes out.

Frequently asked questions

Is root canal treatment painful?

With modern local anaesthesia, most patients say it feels no different from getting a filling. The pain you feel before treatment comes from the infection — the treatment is what relieves it.

Will I need a crown after a root canal?

For back teeth, usually yes. A treated tooth is more brittle, and a crown protects it from cracking under chewing pressure. Front teeth sometimes only need a filling — we'll advise you after the exam.

How long does a root-canal-treated tooth last?

With a well-fitted crown and good home care, a treated tooth can last many years — often a lifetime.

Isn't extraction cheaper?

Removing the tooth costs less on the day, but replacing a missing tooth with an implant or bridge later costs much more. Saving your natural tooth is almost always the better long-term choice.
