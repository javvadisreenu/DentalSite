Wisdom Tooth Pain: Causes, Relief and When to See a Dentist

By Smile Care Dental Hospital · Hyderabad

Wisdom teeth are the last teeth to come in, usually between the ages of 17 and 25. Many people have no trouble with them — but when there isn't enough room in the jaw, they can cause real pain.

Why wisdom teeth hurt

  • Impaction: the tooth is stuck under the gum or bone and pushes against the tooth in front.
  • Pericoronitis: a partly erupted tooth leaves a flap of gum where food and bacteria collect, causing swelling and infection.
  • Decay: wisdom teeth are hard to reach with a brush, so cavities are common.
  • Crowding: pressure on neighbouring teeth can cause aching across the jaw.

Safe relief at home

  • Rinse with warm salt water (half a teaspoon in a glass of water) a few times a day.
  • Use a cold pack on the outside of the cheek for 15 minutes at a time.
  • Brush and floss gently around the area to keep it clean.
  • Over-the-counter pain relief can help — follow the pack instructions and check with a pharmacist if you take other medicines.

Home care can ease symptoms for a short time, but it doesn't fix the cause. Pain that keeps coming back is a sign to get it checked.

See a dentist promptly if you have

  • Swelling of the gum, cheek or jaw
  • Difficulty opening your mouth or swallowing
  • Fever, or a bad taste or pus near the tooth
  • Pain that lasts more than two or three days

Does it need to come out?

Not every wisdom tooth needs removing. An OPG or CBCT scan shows the tooth's position and how close it sits to the nerve. If it is impacted, repeatedly infected or damaging the tooth next to it, wisdom tooth removal is usually the best option — and with modern techniques, most people are back to normal within a week.
