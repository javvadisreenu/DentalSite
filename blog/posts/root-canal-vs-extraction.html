Root Canal or Extraction? How to Decide

By Smile Care Dental Hospital · Hyderabad

When a tooth is badly infected, there are usually two choices: save it with a root canal, or remove it. Both stop the pain — but they lead to very different results over the years. Here's how to think it through.

What each treatment does

A root canal removes the infected pulp from inside the tooth, cleans the canals and seals them. Your natural tooth stays in place, usually protected by a crown.

An extraction removes the whole tooth. The infection goes with it, but you are left with a gap.

Pain

Both are done under local anaesthesia, so neither should hurt during treatment. Recovery after a root canal is usually milder — a little tenderness for two or three days. After an extraction, especially a surgical one, expect a few days of soreness and some swelling.

Cost — today and later

An extraction costs less on the day. But a missing tooth rarely stays a simple gap: neighbouring teeth can drift, the opposite tooth can over-erupt, and the jawbone slowly shrinks. Replacing the tooth with a dental implant or bridge later usually costs far more than the root canal and crown would have.

Long-term results

A root-canal-treated tooth with a good crown can last many years — often a lifetime. Nothing works quite like your own tooth for chewing and keeping the bite stable.

When extraction is the better choice

  • The tooth is cracked below the gum line or split vertically.
  • There isn't enough healthy tooth left to hold a crown.
  • Advanced gum disease has destroyed the bone around it.
  • It is a wisdom tooth with no useful role in chewing.

The bottom line

If the tooth can be saved, a root canal is almost always the better long-term choice. A quick examination and digital X-ray tell us whether your tooth is a good candidate — and if it isn't, we'll talk you through the best way to replace it.
